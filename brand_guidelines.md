# Lumae Corporate Identity Guidelines

## 1. Brand Foundation

Lumae is a working identity for a premium ANZ customer-experience platform. Its brand job is to make operational customer insight feel calm, legible, and useful. The name, palette, and interface language reflect the needs of regulated, service-heavy and digitally mature businesses identified in the concept research; they must not be treated as evidence of trade-mark availability. See the separate market and visual-language research notes for supporting context.

> **Brand promise:** Know what customers feel. Know what to do next.

**Primary line:** Customer clarity, in action.  
**Voice:** Clear, calm, specific, and human.  
**Avoid:** Jargon, exaggerated claims, fear-based copy, and generic “transform your business” language.

## 2. Logo System

The primary mark is an open conversation-and-signal symbol. Its open side represents an ongoing customer dialogue; the rising arcs express clarity emerging from feedback. The supplied mark is symbol-only so that it remains effective in narrow navigation, favicons, and app surfaces. Pair it with a text-rendered **lumae** wordmark using Manrope ExtraBold with tightened tracking.

| Asset | Location | Approved use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary signal mark | `/manus-storage/lumae-logo-mark_b397bb7a.png` | Website header, app favicon direction, social avatar, section label. |
| Wordmark lock-up | Signal mark + `lumae` set in Manrope ExtraBold | Header, footer, website hero, sales collateral. |
| Single-colour mark | Harbour Ink on Salt Paper; Salt Paper on Harbour Ink | Small sizes, printing, low-colour contexts. |

Maintain clear space equal to the diameter of the smallest internal signal circle on all sides. Do not stretch, rotate, outline, add a drop shadow, substitute colours, position the full-colour mark on a busy image, or recreate the mark from text glyphs.

Use Harbour Ink or Cloud for body text surfaces. Tidal Teal is an action colour, not a body-text colour on light backgrounds. Signal Coral should not be used for primary navigation or large paragraph text. The production interface must maintain WCAG AA contrast for normal text and controls.

## 4. Typography

**Manrope** is the primary family because its open forms and excellent small-size clarity support high-density feedback data and a contemporary B2B interface. **DM Mono** is reserved for numerical detail, score labels, cohort sizes, timestamps, and compact annotations.

| Use | Family and weight | Suggested web scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero headline | Manrope 700–800 | 52–72px desktop; 40–48px mobile |
| Section heading | Manrope 700 | 34–46px desktop; 28–34px mobile |
| Body | Manrope 400–500 | 16–18px; 1.55–1.7 line-height |
| Navigation and buttons | Manrope 600–700 | 14–16px |
| Metrics and labels | DM Mono 400–500 | 12–16px |

Use sentence case. Keep headings compact, generally no more than two lines. Use metric numerals only where a data or response context makes them meaningful.

## 5. Layout and Interface Expression

Lumae uses generous outer whitespace, warm paper backgrounds, strong content columns, and tightly controlled cards. Prefer a 12-column desktop grid with a maximum content width of 1,200–1,280px, a comfortable 24px mobile gutter, and an 8px spacing rhythm. Cards are lightly elevated with a 16–24px radius, not heavily outlined. Transitions should be brief, responsive, and limited to opacity and transform.

Data visuals should favour calm panels, clear labels, and Tidal Teal as the principal positive signal. Use Signal Coral only for adverse or attention-required feedback. Do not use visual gauge language that could confuse NPS, CSAT, and CES measures. Labels and timeframes must always accompany score visualisations.

## 6. Illustration, Photography, and Iconography

The brand should favour abstract feedback signals, calm data textures, and real, consented workplace or service-environment photography. Photography should portray real work rather than generic “customer success” stock poses. When representing customers or industries, use a broad and respectful mix of people and contexts; do not imply customer endorsement without written permission.

Icons should be simple rounded-line forms in Harbour Ink, using Tidal Teal only to indicate active states. Avoid emoji-style iconography, gradients, glossy 3D glyphs, and unlabelled decorative charts.

## 7. Copy and Messaging

The product vocabulary is intentionally concrete: **signal**, **response**, **journey**, **action**, **owner**, **trend**, and **recovery**. Describe a feature by the action it makes possible and the decision it supports. For example: “Route detractor feedback to the person who can resolve it,” rather than “AI-powered closed-loop intelligence.”

Pre-launch marketing must not fabricate testimonials, customer logos, review ratings, performance outcomes, certifications, or integration claims. Use descriptive trust framing such as “Built for ANZ businesses” and explain the product’s intended audience and controls without implying current customers, endorsements, or compliance certification.

## 8. Governance

Before launch, conduct domain-availability checks, Australian and New Zealand trade-mark clearance, accessibility testing, privacy review, and legal review of all marketing claims. Any partner or customer logo, case study, quote, or measurable outcome must have a signed approval and a source of record.
